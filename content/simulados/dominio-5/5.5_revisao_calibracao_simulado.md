# Simulado — 5.5 Establish human review workflows and confidence calibration

Domínio 5 (Context Management & Reliability). 6 questões, formato oficial:
múltipla escolha, uma única melhor resposta.

## Q1
A structured-extraction pipeline for invoices, receipts, contract addenda, and
handwritten notes reports 97.0% field accuracy overall (9 errors across 300
accuracy cells spanning 10 fields). Based on this number, the team plans to
cut human review in half. Before doing so, an analyst breaks the same 300
cells down by document type and field, and finds that contract-addendum
documents score only 28.6% on the contract-effective-date field (2 of 7
correct) — a segment the aggregate number never surfaced. What is the most
effective next step?

- **A)** Treat the 97.0% aggregate as sufficient evidence that review can be safely reduced across every document type, since it already exceeds the 95% target the team had set.
- **B)** Break accuracy down by document type and field for every segment, and reduce review only where each relevant segment independently clears the accuracy target.
- **C)** Train a small classifier on prior extractions to predict, per document, whether that specific extraction is likely to be wrong, and route only flagged documents to review.
- **D)** Have the model re-review its own extractions for contract-addendum documents and report an updated confidence for the date field, then decide review levels from that.

## Q2
In that same pipeline's automated bucket (cells with model confidence ≥ 0.90
in production, 156 cells total), the contract-addendum ×
contract-effective-date segment contributes 4 of those cells, and 3 of the 4
are wrong — none of them get reviewed under the current policy. The team has
budget for 40 audit draws to check the automated bucket before trusting it
further. What sampling design most effectively uses that budget?

- **A)** Draw a simple random sample of 40 cells from the whole automated bucket, with no stratification by document type or field.
- **B)** Skip the automated bucket and instead audit 40 cells from the low-confidence review queue, where the model already signals uncertainty.
- **C)** Draw a stratified sample across every document-type × field combination in the automated bucket, guaranteeing at least one draw per combination.
- **D)** Audit only the cells whose confidence sits closest to the 0.90 automation boundary, since a borderline score is most likely to be a mis-threshold.

## Q3
A multi-agent research pipeline logs a self-reported confidence with every
synthesized claim and buckets claims into low, medium, and high confidence.
Validated against a labeled set, low-confidence claims are correct 1 of 2
times (50.0%) and medium-confidence claims are correct 14 of 14 times
(100.0%) — but high-confidence claims are correct only 102 of 104 times
(98.1%), below the medium bucket. What is the most likely root cause of the
medium bucket outscoring the high bucket?

- **A)** A specific segment inside the high-confidence bucket has a systematic, confidence-correlated error — the model is consistently and confidently wrong there, unlike ordinary random noise, which would not produce this kind of inversion.
- **B)** The inversion is ordinary statistical noise given the small n (2) in the low-confidence bucket, and the high-confidence bucket's 98.1% is otherwise unremarkable and needs no further investigation.
- **C)** Raising the confidence threshold that separates "high" from "medium" (e.g., from 0.90 to 0.99) will resolve the inversion, since a stricter bar filters out whatever is causing it.
- **D)** Have the model re-run extraction on the high-confidence claims and report a fresh self-assessed confidence for each; trust whichever of the two scores for a claim is higher.

## Q4
A team selects an automation confidence threshold of 0.98 by testing it
against 180 production cells: 30 cells (16.7%) clear the bar, and all 30 are
correct, so they certify automation at that threshold. When the same 0.98
threshold is later checked against the 12-document labeled validation set
held out earlier, it lets through one known error — a document extracted at
exactly 0.98 confidence that is wrong. What is the most likely explanation,
and what should the team do?

- **A)** Choose thresholds by watching the overall aggregate-accuracy trend as the threshold rises, without checking any labeled validation set — a threshold moved until automation volume looks acceptable is close enough to calibrated.
- **B)** The 30-cell, 0-error result on production is itself sufficient certification, since 30 already meets the minimum sample size the team uses elsewhere to certify a segment.
- **C)** Keep the 0.98 threshold, but additionally spot-check a handful of production cells above it at random on an ongoing basis, without reference to any labeled set.
- **D)** The threshold was chosen and evaluated on the same population (production); select and evaluate the threshold only against the labeled validation set, then freeze it before applying it to production.

## Q5
A customer-support triage agent reports a self-assessed confidence with every
routing decision. The team built a labeled validation set of 200 past
tickets with known-correct routing and measured the agent against it:
high-confidence tickets route correctly 138 of 140 times (98.6%),
medium-confidence 36 of 40 (90.0%), and low-confidence 12 of 20 (60.0%) —
cleanly monotonic, with no inversion. Breaking the high-confidence bucket
down further by ticket category (billing, technical, account) shows every
category individually at or above 98%, each with n ≥ 30. What should the
team do?

- **A)** Route tickets by the agent's self-reported confidence, escalating only medium- and low-confidence tickets to a human, since calibration was measured against a labeled set and every segment inside the automated bucket was independently certified.
- **B)** Never route on self-reported confidence regardless of these numbers, since a model's own confidence score is an unreliable, self-reported signal that should not decide whether a human reviews a ticket.
- **C)** Escalate to a human whenever the customer's message conveys frustration or urgency, regardless of the agent's confidence score.
- **D)** Train a separate classifier on past misrouted tickets to produce a replacement "true" confidence score, instead of using the agent's self-reported one.

## Q6
An extraction pipeline's contract-effective-date field scores 96% on
semantic accuracy (the extracted date is the correct date) — but the string
representation is inconsistent: the same correct date appears as
"12/02/2026", "2026-02-12", and "12 de fevereiro de 2026" across
otherwise-identical documents, breaking a downstream system that expects one
canonical ISO format. Confidence scores on this field are uniformly high
regardless of which format the model chose. What is the most effective fix?

- **A)** Raise the automation confidence threshold for this field, since the field's confidence is already high and a stricter bar will filter out whichever extractions caused the inconsistency.
- **B)** Break accuracy down by document type and field to determine whether this segment needs a higher rate of human review before deciding anything else.
- **C)** Have the model re-review its own output for each document and standardize the date format itself before the extraction is finalized.
- **D)** Add two or three few-shot examples to the extraction prompt showing the input document alongside the desired canonical ISO date format, so the model's output shape becomes consistent.
