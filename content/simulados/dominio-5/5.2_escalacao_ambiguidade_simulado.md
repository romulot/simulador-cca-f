# Simulado — Task Statement 5.2: Design effective escalation and ambiguity resolution patterns

> Domínio 5 · 6 questões · escolha a **única melhor** resposta.

## Q1
A customer-support resolution agent handles 2,300 chats a week. In the 148 chats where the customer typed some form of "I want to talk to a person", the agent first ran three or four lookup tools and re-offered a self-service fix; 61 of those customers had to repeat the request before any handoff happened, and post-chat satisfaction for that group came in 22 points below the weekly average. Every one of the 148 cases was a straightforward issue fully covered by the published policy. Which change is most effective?

- **A)** Have the agent complete its investigation first and attach the full lookup results, so the human inherits a richer and more complete handoff.
- **B)** Put an intent classifier in front of the agent, trained to predict whether the customer wants a human before the agent replies.
- **C)** Escalate only when the request for a person also carries clearly negative wording, which distinguishes a real demand from a passing remark.
- **D)** State in the system prompt that an explicit request for a human is an escalation trigger to be honoured immediately, before any lookup.

## Q2
A structured-data-extraction agent resolves customers before writing rows into the ledger. Its `lookup_customer` tool returns every record matching the name it is given, and in 9% of runs that is two or more records. The agent has been writing to whichever of them was created most recently. Reconciling last quarter's ledger turned up 74 rows written against the wrong customer, and every one of them came from a multi-match run. Which change is most effective?

- **A)** Rank the returned records by string similarity to the supplied name and write to the highest-scoring one, since near-duplicate records differ only slightly.
- **B)** Instruct the agent to treat a multi-match result as ambiguity and ask the customer for an additional identifier, such as an order number.
- **C)** Deduplicate the customer database upstream, so that any name handed to the tool resolves to exactly one surviving record.
- **D)** Have the agent report a self-assessed confidence score for its choice of record, and write the row only when that score is high.

## Q3
A support agent's system prompt tells it to escalate "complex cases" and customers who are "very dissatisfied". A human reviews every escalation. Last month 41% of escalated cases were fully covered by the published policy and were closed by the reviewer in a single message, with no exception granted and no policy question raised. Escalation volume has doubled quarter over quarter, while the share of cases in which the customer asked for a human has stayed flat. Which change addresses the root cause most effectively?

- **A)** Replace the adjectives with the three verifiable triggers — the customer asks for a human, a policy exception or gap, no meaningful progress — plus few-shot examples.
- **B)** Train a case-complexity classifier on last year's ticket history and have the agent escalate whenever the classifier scores a case above a tuned threshold, replacing the prompt's judgement.
- **C)** Move the agent to a larger model, on the grounds that a stronger model judges case complexity and customer dissatisfaction more accurately.
- **D)** Expand the human review capacity and relax its handling target, so that the additional escalation volume is absorbed without backlog.

## Q4
In a multi-agent research system, a policy subagent answers internal questions by quoting the published policy base. An audit of 60 briefs found 12 that stated a commitment with no basis in any policy document: asked whether the company matches a competitor's advertised price, the subagent extended the section covering adjustments on the company's own site by analogy and answered that it does. The subagent's tool scope already reaches the entire policy base, and its quotations on covered topics were accurate in all 60 briefs. Which change is most effective?

- **A)** Widen the subagent's tool scope to the full policy archive, superseded revisions included, so that fewer incoming requests fall outside what it can read.
- **B)** Add a retrieval layer over the policy base and require every sentence of the brief to be grounded in a passage that layer returns.
- **C)** Instruct the subagent that a request the policy does not address is itself an escalation trigger: flag the gap instead of inferring by analogy.
- **D)** Run three independent passes over the policy base for each question and keep the reading that two or more of the passes agree on.

## Q5
A customer writes in capitals, calls the past three weeks "a disgrace" and demands the problem be fixed today. The case takes seven steps — verify the order, check the return window, issue a partial credit, re-ship — and every step is inside the agent's tool scope and explicitly covered by the published policy. The customer has not asked for a human. Across 90 chats of this shape, the human closed the case with the same policy steps 68% of the time, after a median 31 extra hours. Which is the best action?

- **A)** Escalate immediately, because language this negative signals a customer who should be talking to a human rather than to an agent.
- **B)** Acknowledge the frustration and resolve the case, escalating only if the customer then asks for a human.
- **C)** Have the agent rate the case's own complexity and escalate whenever it rates a seven-step resolution as high complexity.
- **D)** Add a deterministic pre-processing hook that escalates any inbound message crossing a threshold of capitalised words.

## Q6
A code-generation agent running in Claude Code hands work back to a human through an `escalate_to_human` tool whose `input_schema` takes a free-text `reason`. Compliance requires that every escalation carry a reason drawn from an auditable vocabulary of three triggers. Across 214 escalations last quarter the field held 63 distinct phrasings, and 38% of them could not be mapped to any of the three, so the quarterly escalation audit could not be produced at all. Which is the best change?

- **A)** Add few-shot examples of well-formed escalations to the system prompt, showing each of the three accepted triggers being used in context.
- **B)** Keep the free-text field and have a nightly report normalise the collected reasons into the three categories before the audit reads them.
- **C)** Add a reviewer model that reads every escalation and classifies the reason the agent stated into one of the three approved triggers.
- **D)** Constrain `reason` to an `enum` of the three triggers in the tool's `strict` `input_schema`, so that no other value can be submitted.
